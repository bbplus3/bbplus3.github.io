About

I spent 18+ years at The Boeing Company, moving from Quality Engineering on the 737 program through workforce analytics and into full-stack ML and BI development. That path wasn't incidental — it gave me something most data scientists don't have: a decade-plus of working inside a regulated, safety-critical environment where a flawed sampling plan or a misread defect trend has real downstream consequences. I owned Boeing's statistical sampling standards, consulted across the quality organization, and built the data infrastructure that executives used to run rate-readiness decisions. Statistical rigor isn't something I apply when convenient; it's the foundation I work from.

Today I build end-to-end ML systems — from raw data through feature engineering, model selection, evaluation, deployment, and the dashboards that make outputs actionable. My recent client work has included nearest-neighbor matching engines designed to operate at enterprise scale, threshold optimization frameworks grounded in measurable business outcomes, and evaluation pipelines that combine ML metrics with business metrics because one without the other tells an incomplete story. I've also built and shipped a full-stack AI career platform, a deployed open-source harm-detection library, and production Streamlit and Shiny applications — each one publicly accessible.

I hold an M.S. in Data Science and an M.S. in Technology Management, alongside a B.S. in Statistics. That combination isn't accidental either: it means I can architect the model, own the project, and translate the result into a format a VP can act on. I work across Python, R, SQL, and JavaScript, and I'm comfortable in the full delivery stack — APIs, databases, cloud deployments, CI/CD, and the documentation that makes a system maintainable after I hand it off.

The problems I'm drawn to are the ones where the cost of being wrong is high and the audience for the answer includes people who will not read a confusion matrix. If you need ML that is both technically defensible and operationally adopted, that's the overlap I've spent nearly two decades building toward.

Open-Source NLP Library

Reveal NLP

Reveal is an open-source Python library for harm and abuse detection in text, combining NLP, linguistic analysis, and cryptanalysis to surface signals that standard tools miss. It detects across 22 categories of propaganda and persuasion technique, grooming and predatory language, self-harm coded language, victim help signals, geographic indicators, and emotional distress — a detection surface well beyond typical sentiment or moderation tools. The technical challenge was building a library-grade architecture (installable via pip, with a live demo endpoint) that is both researcher-extensible and operationally deployable by platform moderators.

"Combines Natural Language Processing, linguistic analysis, and cryptanalysis to identify harmful, coded, or anomalous language — helping researchers, platform moderators, and public safety professionals surface signals that standard tools miss."

Full-Stack AI Platform

Launchpad

Launchpad is a multi-user career management platform I designed and built end to end for consultants and consulting firms, using Python, Flask, SQLite, and the Anthropic Claude API, deployed on Railway. At its core is an assessment engine that maps a consultant's resume, transcripts, and portfolio against a firm's exact skill and technology taxonomy, returning evidence-backed proficiency levels and identifying which practice areas they qualify for. ML Engineering / Business Intelligence

Enterprise Account Matching & Recommendation Pipeline

Designed and built a scalable nearest-neighbor matching engine to match seller target accounts against a large enterprise account universe, where exact matches covered only a fraction of the data and unmatched accounts meant lost revenue opportunities. I engineered business-aware features — geography, industry, revenue segmentation — encoded them for ML, and used FAISS for efficient similarity search, layered over a hierarchical blocking strategy that matched within natural business segments first and fell back to a global search only when necessary. Beyond the matching itself, I built a threshold optimization framework that identified three principled operating points (maximum estimated value, tangent point, annealing point) across a range of event-rate thresholds, combined ML evaluation metrics (precision, recall, F1, ROC-AUC) with business metrics (recommendation coverage, overlap, similarity scores), and delivered Power BI dashboards — including Sankey diagrams — that made complex model outputs actionable for both engineering teams and business stakeholders. The full pipeline ran in Microsoft Fabric using PySpark.

"Rather than relying on arbitrary thresholds, the team could select recommendation cutoffs based on measurable business impact — a threshold optimization framework grounded in estimated revenue, revenue delta, and event rate at each operating point."
